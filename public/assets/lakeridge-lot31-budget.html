What Drives the Range — Big Swing Factors
🔴 Excavation — The Biggest Unknown
A 45% slope with 105 feet of vertical fall means three-level stepped excavation into what may be partially granitic soil. Budget swings $80K–$220K depending on what's in the ground. A geotechnical report before breaking ground is money well spent.
🔴 Glass Wall System
A 22-ft × 2-story custom curtain wall is a major specialty item. Basic systems start at $85K installed. Fully engineered curtain wall with thermal performance, snow load rating, and architectural finish can reach $200K+. Confirm specification with architect before lockdown.
⚠️ Kitchen — The #1 Finish-Level Variable
A "nice" kitchen is $65K. A "great" kitchen is $105K. A "this is why we built here" kitchen is $175K+. Cabinet brand and stone selection are the two biggest levers. Set a hard fixture allowance with your GC early or this line walks on its own.
⚠️ Log Package Pricing
Full-round log packages for custom homes aren't commodity priced. Manufacturer, species, diameter, and finishing all move the number. Get 2-3 quotes from suppliers like Honest Abe, Pioneer Log Homes, and Coventry Log Homes before locking a GC.
⚠️ Septic — Assume Engineered
Grand County soils at elevation rarely pass conventional perc. Budget $42K mid for an engineered system. If you get lucky with perc, it drops to $15K — but that's not the safe planning assumption here.
✓ Primary Bath Finishes
Primary baths have the most direct impact on STR premium pricing. This is a place to spend. The mid-estimate of $48K per primary bath is appropriate for a property targeting $800–$1,200/night on the STR market at Grand Lake.
Where to Spend · Where to Save
Spend Here — It Earns Back
  • The 22-ft glass wall. This IS the building. Don't value-engineer the view everyone is paying for.
  • Hydronic radiant heat. Forces the schedule, protects your logs from drying, and guests feel it. Non-negotiable on a $800/night target.
  • The 56-ft deck. The outdoor fireplace, heaters, and kitchen extend the rental season by 4–6 weeks each spring and fall. That's real revenue.
  • Primary Bath 1. This room photos well and drives bookings. It deserves the spa treatment.
  • Ice + water shield. Do not value-engineer this on a mountain roof. One bad winter and you'll spend more in remediation than you saved.
  • Log chinking and fire-rated stain. Grand County requires it, and it protects the structure long-term.
  • Geotechnical report early. $10–$18K that can save you $80K in change orders during excavation.
Save Here — Doesn't Hurt the Experience
  • Guest bath finishes (shared bath for 2 queen rooms). Builder-grade tile, basic vanity. Guests don't notice vs. high-end here.
  • Media lounge casework — custom built-ins are expensive. A well-spec'd floating shelf + AV rack looks intentional and costs 60% less.
  • Garage interior. Ski lockers are functional. The garage doesn't need to be finished beyond utility.
  • Workout room equipment — not in the construction budget anyway. Buy it when the room is done.
  • Landscaping. High-elevation revegetation is mostly native grasses. Don't over-landscape something nature will maintain.
  • Bunk level. Shell it and finish it in Year 2 when cash flows. Smart sequencing, not compromise.
  • Outdoor lighting — one well-placed architectural fixture beats eight generic ones. Less is more at 8,400 ft.
