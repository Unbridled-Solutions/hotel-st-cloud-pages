Part 1 — High Stakes Audience Strategy

1 Know Your Audience

  • Identify their pain points — risks, broken systems
  • Know their motivations
  • Are they analytical or intuitive decision-makers?
  • Are they risk-averse or risk-tolerant?
Your Notes

2 Why Are They Here?

  • What pressures are they under?
  • What are they afraid of?
  • Are they here because they want to be — or have to be?
Your Notes

3 How Does Your Message Solve Their Problem?

  • Make the problem undeniable
  • Position your idea as the bridge
  • Speak their language — not yours
Your Notes

4 What Do You Want Them to Do?

  • Land on one clear ask
  • Make the stakes crystal clear — benefits of action, cost of inaction
Your Notes

5 What Is the One Big Idea?

Formula: Point of view + what's at stake

A single sentence containing a verb — memorable, understandable, shareable.

6 Everything Supports the Big Idea

Every slide, story, data point, and visual must connect back to your one big idea. If it doesn't serve it — cut it.

Part 2 — Story Frameworks

The Story Arc

What Is → Tension & Contrast → What Could Be → Inspire → Direct (Next Right Actions)
  • Three Acts: Set up the world → Introduce the conflict → Resolution or Cliffhanger
  • Give characters the audience cares about
  • Build toward the "Whoa" moment

Part 3 — Data Story

Turn Data Into Decisions

  • Data needs to arrive as a recommendation, not a raw number
  • Visualize meaning — show the outcome, not just the chart
"Because [data]… we must [action]… in order to [outcome]."
Your Data Points
Part 4 — Design Your Story

Slide Design Principles

  • Choose color with purpose — not default themes
  • One idea per slide — ruthless density control
  • Use animation only when it earns its place
  • If a slide can't be understood in 3 seconds, redesign it
