OrangeDoor in Europe. Where we actually stand.

A SWOT, then a ranking against the European live-event field. Honest, not a cheer sheet. Written so we can decide what to sell this year.

OrangeDoor, an Unbridled company · August 2026 · Sources: merger coverage, B Corp profile, Unbridled public facts, internal notes. Not a formal valuation.

OrangeDoor alone Upper-middle. About a 7 / 10.

Stronger than most London independents on values, exhibitions, and now US reach. Weaker than the globals on name recognition, buying power, and continental offices.

OrangeDoor + Unbridled The rank jumps.

A transatlantic independent with a real US production bench. Still not MCI. We do not need to be.

Do not rank us against Jack Morton, MCI, or George P. Johnson on size. Those are holding-company machines. Different sport. Rank us against independent and mid-market B2B live-event and exhibition agencies in the UK and Europe.

Strengths

S

  • Twenty-five years. Same DNA as Unbridled. Elizabeth built it. Andrew ran the commercial side.
  • B Corp since November 2022. Score 104 against a median near 51. Majority female-led. Buyers can verify that.
  • Wider than a typical London production house: strategy, creative, tech, production, logistics, exhibitions, content. Tech, finance, and HR are the lanes. We bring pharma, food and beverage, and big-room production.
  • The merger is the real asset. Five US offices overnight. Their exhibitions plus our media, motion, and show-call muscle. A New England job already shipped together. That is not a press release.
  • Purpose is not a slide. B Corp plus 20/20/60. Sustainability committees will shortlist you before they shortlist a holding-company shop that rented a green logo.

W

  • Europe still mostly means London. Delivery into Belgium, France, Germany is not the same as a Frankfurt or Amsterdam office with a local book. MCI and GPJ win those RFPs on "we already live there."
  • The brand is quiet. Industry press covered the merger. The average European procurement lead has heard of Jack Morton. They have not heard of OrangeDoor. That costs the first cut.
  • Integration is unfinished. Two cultures, two stacks, two P&Ls, one name on the door. If a client feels the seam, the merger story becomes a risk.
  • Scale versus the globals. We will lose the 10,000-person user conference that wants one agency in 40 cities. Stop chasing those unless we are a specialist workstream.

O

  • US clients who need Europe, and EU clients who need the States. That is the only list that matters this year. One team. One invoice. No third-party "local partner."
  • Exhibitions and live in one house. Most Europeans split the booth and the general session. We can stop that split.
  • Middle East, then Asia. Do not open three offices. Win two flagship clients and follow them.
  • Proof, not poetry. Retention, reuse, B Corp, 20/20/60, the tools already shown. That is how we beat a prettier deck.

T

  • Procurement will treat us as "small UK shop plus US vendor." If we do not show one operating system, they will unbundle us.
  • Holding companies will copy the purpose line and outspend us on the pitch.
  • Post-merger drift. If London feels acquired, or Denver treats London as a satellite, the thing that made this deal work dies in 18 months.
  • Andrew and Elizabeth concentration. The relationship is the deal. A succession plan is not optional.

What I would do

  1. Stop selling "European agency." Sell the transatlantic independent that still answers the phone.
  2. Pick ten named accounts that already buy on both sides of the ocean. That is the year.
  3. Publish one shared show operating system so the seam disappears in the room.
  4. Do not open a continental office until a client pays for the first year of it.
