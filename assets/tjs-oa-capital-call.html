Bas will not fund the capital call

T.J.'s Wood Products Acquisition LLC. Operating Agreement dated 1 March 2025. The 5% is Bas Weijmans. The OA can demand more capital. It does not take his units if he says no.

This page was prepared by MC as a starting point for the partners. It has not been reviewed by a licensed Colorado attorney. Do not treat it as legal advice. Birch Grove / Nick should confirm process, consent, and any Award Agreements before anyone writes Bas or moves the cap table.

Verdict

If Member Consent under §7.9.8 was validly obtained, Bas can be required to put in more money. His share of a $180,000 call at 5% is $9,000, not $11,000. If he still refuses, this OA does not forfeit the 5%, does not silently dilute him, and does not force a buyout. The practical paths are: document the call, let willing members put more in (optional, new units), have the company borrow the hole, or (higher risk) treat a missed required call as a Withdrawal Event and decide whether to buy him at fair market value.

What the OA actually says

  • §4.2(d). No Member has to put in more capital except under §7.9.8.
  • §7.9.8. The company may demand required additional capital, and set the form, only with Member Consent (majority of Units). U&I at 94% can carry a meeting vote by itself if a meeting is properly called.
  • §3.9. Action by written consent without a meeting needs all Members entitled to vote. If Bas never signed a written consent, you needed a meeting, not a paper he refused to sign.
  • §7.9.7. Optional extra capital is a different tool. Willing members can put more in. That is how you change the table without pretending he forfeited.
  • §4.1. New Units are allocated proportionate to the value contributed. The Manager revises the Schedule. There is no automatic formula that shrinks a refuser.
  • §7.8.1.2. Manager may borrow from banks, Members, or affiliates instead of calling capital.
  • Article 8 / Exhibit B(f). A Member's "other violation of this Agreement" can be a Withdrawal Event. Then he becomes a Transferee (economics, no vote). The company may buy at FMV within 120 days. It is not required to. There is no missed-call penalty clause.

Options if he still says no

1. Prove the call, then ask again in writing
OA hook: §7.9.8 + meeting notice §3.6–3.8. Cap table: no change yet.

First question is process. Was this a required call with majority consent at a meeting, or an email asking for money? If the paper is thin, fix the paper before you talk remedy. Then send Bas a short notice: amount ($9,000 if 5% of $180k), due date, form (cash), and that this is a §7.9.8 required contribution.

Counsel confirms: minutes or unanimous written consents for this call and any earlier ones in the succession.

2. Treat his check as optional from everyone else (cleanest cap-table move)
OA hook: §7.9.7 optional contributions + §4.1 new Units. Cap table: Bas stays at 50 Units; his % falls as others add capital.

Willing members (almost certainly U&I, maybe Scott) fund the $180,000 or the $9,000 hole. Issue new Units only to those who write checks. Bas keeps his 50 Units. He is diluted by math, not by a fight. Example if U&I alone puts in the full $180,000 at the original $425 per Unit: 424 new Units, Bas drops from 5.0% to about 3.5%. Exact price per Unit is a Member Consent item. Do not invent it in the room.

Colorado risk: low if consent is documented. This is the partner-first path.

3. Company borrows the hole
OA hook: §7.8.1.2. Cap table: unchanged.

Bank, member loan, or affiliate loan. Useful if you need cash this week and do not want to reopen Units. Does not solve a partner who will not fund the next call either. Counsel should paper rate and security so it does not look like a hidden distribution.

4. Withdrawal Event, then decide whether to buy
OA hook: Exhibit B(f) + §8.4. Cap table: Bas becomes Transferee (no vote). Buy is optional at FMV.

Only if a required §7.9.8 call was valid and he defaulted. "Other violation" is broad and untested in this document. If you go here, he loses management/vote. You may purchase within 120 days at fair market value. You are not required to. There is no free grab of the 5%.

Colorado risk: moderate. Do not send this letter without Nick. A Dutch individual and a Colorado LLC is enough reason to get the process right.

5. Leave him. Absorb the $9,000
OA hook: §4.2(d) and the missing penalty clause. Cap table: status quo.

Cheap this round. Expensive if this is one of a succession of calls and he sits out all of them. Only a pause, not a structure.

What this OA does not do

  • Does not forfeit Bas's 5%.
  • Does not contain a dilution formula for a missed call.
  • Does not let the Manager demand capital alone.
  • Does not force a squeeze-out.
  • Does not make Jason Bowen, or Unbridled Companies, a "Managing Partner of the investor group" as a defined title. Day-to-day Manager in Exhibit B and on the signature page is Jason Bowen. §7.4 also says Unbridled Companies LLC cannot be removed as Manager during Stan's life except by court order. Those two sentences conflict. Counsel should say which controls before anyone signs as Manager on a capital-call notice.
  • §12.11 mentions Award Agreements. They are not in this PDF. If Bas has a separate award, that paper may change the story. Get it before you write him.
