The call

Build a living show ledger. Do not put AI on top of the workbook.

The LPL file is seven sheets, hundreds of lines, #REF! cells, last-write chaos waiting to happen. Rentman and event CRMs will not replace this. Our wedding planner will not either. New product. New data model. Voice is real if every money change shows a confirm card first.

What the LPL book actually taught

The object is a show, not a list

Client LPL. Meeting Focus. San Diego Convention Center. August 9–12, 2026. 7,000 attendees. 10 Unbridled staff. Creative and Production are two businesses on one invoice. Deposits already match the contract total to the penny: $5,863,741.06 in, three draws.

The pain is variance over time

Contract $5.86M. Pre-show 8.3.26 is $5.45M. That is $411k under, mostly because the 5% contract escalators and Focus Live sat on the contract column and never landed in working. Labor blew +$1.12M. Breakout gear dropped $536k. Video media dropped $208k. On-site $975k became $324k billed to master. The tool has to make that story obvious without opening seven tabs.

Daily rooms drive money

GS is one room. Breakouts are many rooms × days. Labor is a 10-hour day, OT at 1.5 after 10. Change Thursday from 18 rooms to 14 and labor, gear, and the 20% fee all move. Qty × unit × days is the atom. A flat line-item list cannot do this.

Many inputs, one truth

Creative (Robertson). Productions (Alliant, SiteStream, Unbridled labor). Discounts pre vs post. Billed-to-master. Client signature block. Additional Events sheet is empty. Additional Discounts is a stub. The next person who emails a new xlsx will overwrite somebody. That is the product problem.

What I would not build

TemptationWhy it dies
AI chat sitting on the Excel file Formulas already have #REF!. Voice plus a broken book is a faster way to invent numbers.
Reuse the wedding / HSC planner Guest-count tax badges are a different sport. No rooms-by-day, no labor OT, no 20% fee base, no contract/working/actual, no vendors.
One Cloudflare KV blob, last write wins Fine for Stan + Reili. Fatal when Productions and Creative edit at once. A $5.8M show is not a single JSON document.
Buy Rentman / Cvent / a rental OS Those are inventory and registration. This is an agency client budget plus recon. We would still keep a side spreadsheet.
Voice that writes money with no confirm Whisper is ~8–12% word error in the real world. “Fifteen” vs “fifty.” STT already mangles our names. Unconfirmed voice on a budget is malpractice.

The product

Show Budget OS. One URL per show. Four columns that never leave the screen: Contract, Working, Actual, Variance. A room calendar at the top. A voice button that proposes, never commits.

1 · Build

Clone last year. Drop in this year’s room grid and dates. AI maps the old book onto the new skeleton. Humans accept or reject each mapped section. Empty Actuals. Contract locked after client sign.

2 · Live

Producers edit their lane. Vendors upload a package. Daily room counts change. Working moves. Contract stays. Everyone sees who touched what, when.

3 · Recon

Actuals get entered or matched from invoices. Deposits vs remaining. Billed-to-master split out. Final recon invoice is a view, not a new file.

Data model (this is the whole game)

If we get this wrong, the UI will not save us.

ObjectWhat it holds
Show Client, name, venue, dates, attendees, staff count, currency, fee rules, year, cloned_from
Snapshot Contract / Working / Actual. Working is live. Contract is frozen after sign. Actual fills during recon.
Space General Session, named breakout, additional room. Calendar of days on / off. Headcount optional.
Line qty, unit cost, days or hours, discount mode (none / pre / post), vendor, GL code, billed_to_master, taxable
Labor rule 10-hour day default. OT 1.5 after 10. Load-in / show / strike as day types, not mystery multipliers.
Fee 20% Productions on equipment + on-site + partner labor. Creative fee as its own rule. Never silently bake fees into unit cost.
Deposit Invoice date, received date, amount. Remaining = current working (or contract, your choice, labeled) minus received.
Event log Every change: who, when, field, old, new, source (ui / voice / import). This is how you sleep.

Daily room counts sit on Space, not on every line. Lines subscribe to a space and a day pattern (“all show days,” “load-in only,” “Thu–Sat”). Change the calendar, money moves. That is the adjustment you described.

Voice. Yes, with a gate.

Talk to propose. Tap to commit.

Hold to talk on phone or laptop. Whisper (or Groq) to text. A small model turns the sentence into a structured patch: target show, target lines, old value, new value. The screen shows a card: “Set Thursday breakouts from 18 to 14. Working production drops an estimated $X. Fee drops $Y. Contract unchanged.” You say yes, or tap yes. Then it writes.

Good commands: “Add two Alliant A2s Thursday.” “Zero the Duarte workshop.” “Clone 2026 Focus into 2027 and apply 5%.” “What’s over contract in labor?”

Bad commands we refuse: anything that changes Contract after lock, anything that deletes a section without naming it, any dollar figure the STT is not sure about. If the transcript says fifty and the line was fifteen, we ask.

We already know STT mangles names. Vendor and people names come from a show dictionary, not from raw speech. “Alliant” and “SiteStream” and “Cheryl Trinidad” are buttons the model must pick from.

People, passwords, rights

A shared password on a URL is not enough. One leak and the whole book is public. Named people. Magic link or a personal PIN. Roles per show, not per company forever.

RoleCan doCannot
Owner (Stan / finance) Everything. Lock contract. Close recon. Clone year. —
Producer / PM Edit Working in their sections. Voice. Upload vendor files. Unlock contract. Change fee rules. Delete the show.
Creative lead Creative & Content Working only. Production lines. Deposits.
Vendor (Alliant, etc.) Upload a package into a holding area. See only their lines. See Unbridled fee, other vendors, client deposits.
Client (optional) View summary + variance notes. Comment. Edit. See internal cost vs sell if we mark it internal.
Viewer Read Working and variance. Write.

Vendor upload is a first-class path. They drop a PDF or xlsx. AI maps it to a proposed patch. A producer accepts. That replaces the email-Excel loop without giving them the whole book.

Year to year

Clone is not “save as.” Clone copies the skeleton: spaces, line names, vendors, fee rules, room types. It zeros Actuals. It keeps last year’s Working as the new Contract candidate, with an optional 5% escalator (the LPL book already thinks this way, even if the math on those two 5% lines does not equal 5% of the subtotal). You then paint this year’s calendar. AI flags lines that existed last year and have no owner this year.

LPL Focus 2026 becomes the template for LPL Focus 2027 in one action. Casey’s, or the next pharma, gets its own family of shows. Templates live at the client level, not as a global “one budget to rule them all.”

Where it lives

Cloud
  • Cloudflare D1 (SQL) for shows, lines, rooms, users, log
  • R2 for source workbooks and vendor invoices
  • Durable Object per show for presence and row-level last-write (not a 2MB KV blob)
  • KV only for sessions and pins
AI jobs (narrow)
  • Import: xlsx → proposed schema map
  • Voice: transcript → patch card
  • Drift: daily “what moved more than X%”
  • Recon: invoice line → budget line match
  • Clone: last year → this year with a checklist

The math never lives in the model. Qty × days × unit, OT, fees, billed-to-master, tax: all deterministic in code. AI is not allowed to invent a total.

What research said, in one minute

Production budgets fail on overtime, rigging, and room count, not on missing a logo line. Labor is paid for load-in days, not just show days. Conference AV is rooms × days. Collaborative spreadsheets that last-write a whole document lose work; last-write at the line is fine. Whisper is good enough for commands and bad enough that money needs a confirm. Off-the-shelf event software is built for planners and rental houses. Nobody sells “agency working budget with voice and recon” as a product. That is why this is ours.

Build order

  1. Week 1. Schema + LPL import. Read this workbook into D1. Prove Contract / Working / Variance on one page. No voice yet. No multi-user yet.
  2. Week 2. Room calendar. Change a day’s room count and watch labor, gear, and the 20% fee move. This is the demo that sells the tool internally.
  3. Week 3. People. Named logins, roles, audit log. Vendor upload bay.
  4. Week 4. Voice + clone. Confirm cards. Duplicate 2026 → 2027. Drift digest.
  5. After first live show. Recon matching and the client-facing summary.

Do not start with a pretty empty template. Start by swallowing the LPL book so the team recognizes their own numbers on day one.
