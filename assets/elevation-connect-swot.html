Strengths

S

  • US voice, not a mystery offshore logo. Public copy: American operators, calls answered in the USA. Brick in Colorado and Michigan. Virtual bench they say covers 48 states. For a mid-market brand that got burned on accent or night-shift quality, that is the product.
  • Vertical muscle you can see in the job board, not just the brochure. Indeed listings this month: education admissions / application support, guest service, bilingual Spanish account work. Reviews name Five9, Salesforce, ImageNow. That is a real stack, not “we do CX.”
  • Purpose is not a slide. 20/20/60 with the Emergent / Unbridled family. Social-good page names Peaceful Hugs, Denver Rescue Mission, Ronald McDonald House Detroit, Love 146, M Family Foundation. Buyers who care about vendor character can check this. Most small BPOs cannot.
  • Named operators in public directories: Brad Krinhop, Managing Partner; Eric Davidson, COO. Founded 2013 (Clutch / RocketReach). LinkedIn still lives at company/unbridled-connect, which is the honest lineage.
  • Mix they actually offer: inbound, outbound, chat / email / text, back office, seasonal / burst, onshore plus a nearshore / offshore option they name (Caribbean, Mexico, Philippines). A small shop that can burst is useful. A small shop that pretends it is all of those at once is not. See Weaknesses.

W

  • The website is the loudest weakness. Homepage claims Fortune 500 to start-ups, “savings guaranteed or we pay the difference,” and “simply the best analytics & intelligence BPO firm in the country.” None of that is evidenced on the page. Dead “Button” links. Where We Live still has “Slide title / Write your caption here.” LinkedIn still says Unbridled Connect. Footer is 2025. It reads unfinished.
  • No public proof. Clutch unclaimed, no reviews, no case studies, no named clients, no industries filled in. Min project and hourly rate undisclosed. A procurement person who starts on Clutch sees a ghost.
  • Two addresses in public records and they do not match. Site and phone: 9250 E. Costilla, Greenwood Village. Clutch and RocketReach: 1400 Josephine, Denver (Josephine Mansion). Pick one HQ sentence. Split addresses look like a move that never got cleaned up.
  • Headcount is a range, not a number. Clutch 50–249. RocketReach 112 listed / 62 “employed.” Site says “hundreds.” Indeed culture notes small teams and few permanent seats. Do not quote hundreds until the shop will stand behind it in an RFP.
  • RocketReach prints $68.2 million revenue. Treat that as directory noise until Connect confirms it. A 50–250 person boutique at that number would be an outlier. Curiosity, not a claim.
  • Wage story vs brand story. Open jobs around $14–$15/hour remote. Fine for seasonal admissions. Hard to square with “Fortune 500 CX” and “state-of-the-art analytics.” Buyers can see both tabs.
  • Indeed is mixed (3.8 / 15). Flexibility and training show up. So do holiday layoffs, tenured reps expected to know every client, and a couple of “run away” reviews. Public. A client who Googles the employer brand will find it.

O

  • Mid-market US brands that want English, same-day overlap, and a human on the phone, not a Manila night shift. World Connection already owns a clean version of this story on the nearshore side. Elevation can own the US-voice, owner-led, purpose version if the site stops claiming the whole planet.
  • Education admissions is already in the work. Seasonal, deadline spikes, application support. That is a lane a 100-person shop can dominate in Colorado / remote US. Do not bury it under “and more.”
  • Unbridled hospitality and live events already take guest calls. Hotel St. Cloud, Fremont, Standard Oil, TJ’s. Connect should be the default overflow, not a bake-off with a national answering service. Same for Unbridled Wealth / Insurance if those books need licensed-adjacent intake (confirm licenses before you sell it).
  • Bilingual Spanish is on the job board. Mid-market healthcare-adjacent, education, and hospitality will pay for that without going full nearshore.
  • Claim Clutch. Three case studies (education, hospitality, one other). One quote-in-48-hours path. That is how World Connection and the clean boutiques get found. Elevation is invisible there today.

T

  • Nearshore LATAM shops publicly sell 50–70% savings vs onshore. If Elevation leads with “cheaper than in-house, guaranteed,” they will get priced into a fight they cannot win on labor, and they will look naive on the guarantee.
  • AI deflection is eating Tier-1 password resets and FAQs. The remaining work is the hard stuff: admissions exceptions, guest recovery, sales with judgment. If the site still sells “chatbots and self service” as the headline, they look like every 2023 pitch deck.
  • A buyer lands on elevationconnect.com, hits a placeholder caption and a savings guarantee, and hires the shop with SOC 2 on the homepage. The work never gets in the room.
  • Name split. Unbridled Connect on LinkedIn, Elevation Connect on the web, Josephine vs Greenwood Village. Diligence flags that in the first hour.
  • Campaign concentration. Public reviews mention clients getting bought and seats disappearing. Boutique BPOs die when two logos leave. The site does not show a book of work, so we cannot tell if that risk is real. Assume it until they publish it.

Market strategies at this size

Recommendation, not a menu. At 50–250 people you cannot be onshore, nearshore, offshore, Fortune 500, start-up, analytics leader, and free consultant. Pick the work that already shows up in hiring and reviews.

  1. Primary ICP: US mid-market education and enrollment. Admissions, application support, deadline burst, bilingual as an add. Seasonal is a feature. Price on quality and overlap, not vs Manila.
  2. Second ICP: US hospitality and guest recovery. Start with Unbridled’s own hotels and restaurants as the proof account, then sell the same motion to independent hotels and attraction brands that will not give the front desk to a mega BPO.
  3. Do not lead with nearshore or “savings guaranteed.” Offer nearshore only after the US program is specified. A guarantee you cannot audit is a reason to lose the RFP.
  4. Sell overflow and surge, not “take the whole center.” Boutiques win when the client keeps a small in-house core and needs nights, season, or a second language. That matches the Indeed story.
  5. Fix the storefront before you buy ads. Claim Clutch. One HQ. Three case studies. Kill placeholders. Then run LinkedIn and education-conference presence. Traffic onto this site today wastes the click.
