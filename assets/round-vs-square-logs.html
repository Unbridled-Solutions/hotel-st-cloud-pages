1. What Sunday’s 36% actually is

To cut the biggest possible square from a round log, the diameter has to be the diagonal of that square. That is side × √2. Waste is always 36% of the parent log at that exact size. It is 1 − 2/π. It does not change with log size if you take the biggest square the log will give you.

Finished square Min round (geometry) Waste at min Shop log (no wane) Waste at shop size
12 × 12 17.0" 36% 18–19" 43–49%
14 × 14 19.8" 36% 21–22" 43–48%
14 × 14 from a 26" log 26.0" 63% overkill you throw away most of the tree

Bark, taper, sweep, and saw kerf eat the corners. Do not buy 19.8 for a clean 14. Do not buy 26 unless you want a 16–18 inch square, or you are using the extra wood for slabs, beams, and decking.

2. How $1.09M is built. Waste and mill are both in it.

This is the part that can look like a conflict. It is not. You do not buy 13,000 LF of 14 inch square, then add 36% more logs, then add mill. You buy 13,000 LF of 22 inch round. The mill cuts a 14 inch square out of each stick. Waste is wood you already paid for in the fat log. Mill is a second check.

3. The three cost drivers, ranked

1. Parent log size (the real money)

Colorado mill list does not climb smoothly. 15–18" peeled is $42/LF. 19–22" is $51. Then 23–25" is $100. Peeling alone on that last band is $66/LF. 24–26" house logs are special order. That jump is bigger than mill labor and bigger than the 36% waste.

2. Waste (36% is the floor, not the shop number)

Inscribed 14 from 19.8: 36% waste. Shop 14 from 22: about 48% of the parent is slab. From 26: 63%. Slabs are not worthless (siding, decking, firewood) but they are not wall logs. Credit them cheap, not 1:1.

3. Mill labor to make it square

Custom bandsaw work in 2026 runs about $0.35–$1.00 per board foot, or $75–$155/hour. Four faces on a house log, plus kerf. Hand-hewn faces add about $0.75–$1.75/LF. For a 14" house log, mill-to-square is roughly $5–$14 per linear foot. That is real money on 13,000 LF ($65k–$180k), but it is still smaller than buying a 26" parent instead of a 22".

Stacking labor is mixed. Square logs bear flat and can go up faster. Corners (dovetail vs saddle) and settling detailing still have to be paid. Do not count on a big field-labor save to fund the bigger tree.

7. Recommendation

14 inch square is sold. Deliver it from 21–22 inch parents (row B). Kit plus stack: about $1.09 million. Row C is the same 14 from 23–25 inch logs: about $1.72 million. Same wall. Wrong tree. D is 12 inch at the same eave (~15,200 LF): about $1.07 million. Not the product, and not a real save if height has to match.

Holding 13,000 LF on a 12 inch wall understated it. Same height needs about 17% more courses. Cheaper 18" parent, more sticks. Net lands next to the round mix.

8. What this is not

  • Not a TJ’s bid. Ask the mill for 13,000 LF of 21–22" peeled, then 14×14 four-square, kiln or air-dry, species, slab credit, and a stack-crew number.
  • Not roof, windows, chink, stain, or GC.
  • If “13,000” was house square footage, not log linear feet, this whole model is the wrong denominator. Confirm that once.
  • Row D (12") is recast at the same Pence eave (12'-2¼"). More courses, ~15,200 LF, not 13,000. Rows A–C stay at 13,000 LF.
