1. What Sunday’s 36% actually is

To cut the biggest possible square from a round log, the diameter has to be the diagonal of that square. That is side × √2. Waste is always 36% of the parent log at that exact size. It is 1 − 2/π. It does not change with log size if you take the biggest square the log will give you.

14 × 14 from a 26" log 26.0" 63% overkill you throw away most of the tree

Bark, taper, sweep, and saw kerf eat the corners. Do not buy 19.8 for a clean 14. Do not buy 26 unless you want a 16–18 inch square, or you are using the extra wood for slabs, beams, and decking.

2. The three cost drivers, ranked

1. Parent log size (the real money)

Colorado mill list does not climb smoothly. 15–18" peeled is $42/LF. 19–22" is $51. Then 23–25" is $100. Peeling alone on that last band is $66/LF. 24–26" house logs are special order. That jump is bigger than mill labor and bigger than the 36% waste.

2. Waste (36% is the floor, not the shop number)

Inscribed 14 from 19.8: 36% waste. Shop 14 from 22: about 48% of the parent is slab. From 26: 63%. Slabs are not worthless (siding, decking, firewood) but they are not wall logs. Credit them cheap, not 1:1.

3. Mill labor to make it square

Custom bandsaw work in 2026 runs about $0.35–$1.00 per board foot, or $75–$155/hour. Four faces on a house log, plus kerf. Hand-hewn faces add about $0.75–$1.75/LF. For a 14" house log, mill-to-square is roughly $5–$14 per linear foot. That is real money on 13,000 LF ($65k–$180k), but it is still smaller than buying a 26" parent instead of a 22".

Stacking labor is mixed. Square logs bear flat and can go up faster. Corners (dovetail vs saddle) and settling detailing still have to be paid. Do not count on a big field-labor save to fund the bigger tree.

5. Same house, all-in. Three builds.

House size is Eagles Landing: 10,900 SF on the construction tracker. Grand County. Same roof, same kitchen, same mechanicals, same finishes. Only the wall-log system changes. Log package is about 9% of a house this size, so a $100k wall delta barely moves the total. Crossing 23 inches does.

Turnkey range is luxury mountain log, not builder grade: about $550–$750 per SF. Mid is $650. That is Grand Lake, not a 1,000 SF cabin kit.

Not in these totals as extras: well depth surprises, engineered septic, a second garage wing, or finish creep in the kitchen. Those swing more than the wall logs if you stay under 23 inches. GC 15–20% and 10% contingency are inside the $550–$750/SF band.

6. Recommendation

If the next house is 13,000 LF of wall and you want a 14 inch square look, spec a 21–22 inch parent, not 26. Log line: about $0.77 million vs $0.67 million round. All-in on 10,900 SF: about $7.2 million vs $7.1 million. A $100k premium on a $7 million house. Cross 23 inches and the same house is about $7.8 million.

Drop to a 12 inch square from an 18 inch parent and the package can come in under the round mix, around $0.63 million. You give up mass, shadow line, and a lot of the “this is a real log house” feel that Eagles Landing has. Wildfire code in Grand County wants solid wood ≥ 8 inches, so 12 still clears. It just will not read like 16–24 round.

Do not buy 26 inch logs to make 14 inch squares unless the mill will credit the slabs at something close to timber value, or you need 26s for a different reason (ridge, posts, great-room). Otherwise you are paying the scarcity price to throw most of the tree on the slab pile.

7. What this is not

  • Not a TJ’s bid. Ask the mill for 13,000 LF of 21–22" peeled, then 14×14 four-square, kiln or air-dry, species, and slab credit.
  • Not installed wall. Scribe, crane, settling, chink, stain sit on top of these numbers.
  • If “13,000” was house square footage, not log linear feet, this whole model is the wrong denominator. Confirm that once.
  • If the square house must match Eagles Landing wall height, a 14" square needs more courses than a 20" average round. That would raise LF, and the premium, further. This page holds LF constant because that is how you stated both houses.
