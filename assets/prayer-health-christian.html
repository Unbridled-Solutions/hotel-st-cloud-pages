Does labeling it Christian change the outcome?

Follow-up to the first brief. Same question, now through a biblical and Pentecostal lens: Randy Clark, Bill Johnson, Bethel, and the one peer-reviewed study that actually measured hands-on Christian prayer.

Straight read The big distant-prayer trials were already Christian. Born-again intercessors. Prayer to the God of the Bible. That did not move hospital charts. What the Bible actually describes is usually in-person, with touch, in Jesus' name. That design has one small peer-reviewed field study, and it is in Randy Clark's network. Bethel has thousands of testimonies and, as of 2022 reporting, no published medically verified miracle from its own rooms. Scripture itself never promised that every prayer for healing ends in a physical cure.

1. The large trials were already Christian

This is the part that surprises people. "Make it Christian" was already the protocol.

Byrd, 1988. San Francisco CCU

Born-again Christians prayed to the Judeo-Christian God for 393 coronary-care patients. Byrd reported a better hospital course. Smaller, older, and the signal did not hold when the bigger trials ran.

STEP. Benson, Harvard, 2006

1,802 bypass patients. Prayer provided by three Christian groups in the United States for 14 days. Blinded Christian prayer: complications 52% versus 51%. No benefit. The group that knew they were being prayed for did worse (59%). Mortality the same.

Cochrane 2009 folded these in. Distant intercessory prayer, including explicitly Christian prayer, did not show a clear effect on death or general clinical state. Relabeling the same design "biblical" does not change that result. James 5 is not a stranger in another city reading a first name off a list.

2. What Scripture actually describes

In person, with touch, in Jesus' name

James 5:14-15: the elders of the church pray over the sick person and anoint with oil. Mark 6 and Acts: laying on of hands. Jesus healed people who were in the room. That is proximal prayer, not a blinded envelope.

Scripture also refuses a vending-machine reading. Paul left Trophimus sick at Miletus (2 Timothy 4:20). His own thorn was not removed (2 Corinthians 12:7-9). Timothy is told to take a little wine for his stomach (1 Timothy 5:23). A Christian research brief that skips those verses is not being biblical. It is being promotional.

3. The Clark study. Hands-on Pentecostal prayer

This is the paper people mean when they ask about Randy Clark.

STEPP. Candy Gunther Brown, Southern Medical Journal, 2010

Indiana University religious-studies researcher. Field study in rural Mozambique with Iris Ministries (Heidi and Rolland Baker) and Global Awakening (Randy Clark). Not Bethel. Same charismatic stream: in-person prayer, touch, expectation of healing.

24 consecutive people who said they had hearing and/or vision problems. Handheld audiometer and vision charts before and after prayer. Hearing improved (p less than 0.003). Vision improved (p less than 0.02). Two hearing subjects dropped the threshold at which they could detect sound by 50 decibels. Three vision subjects went from 20/400 or worse to 20/80 or better. One woman who could not see two fingers at one foot counted five fingers after less than a minute of prayer and read the 20/125 line.

Brown wrote that the size of the change was larger than typical suggestion and hypnosis studies. She called for more work, especially where glasses and hearing aids are scarce.

What that study is not

Not a randomized controlled trial. No untreated control group. Consecutive cases, before-and-after, in a revival setting. n = 24. Field conditions. It cannot prove the mechanism was supernatural, and it cannot be stacked against STEP as if they were the same experiment. It is the strongest peer-reviewed measurement we have of the thing Clark actually does: proximal Christian prayer, not distant lists.

4. Bill Johnson and Bethel

Testimonies, not a published trial

Bethel Redding runs healing rooms, a School of Supernatural Ministry, and a large public testimony archive. Bill Johnson teaches that healing is part of what Jesus paid for. That is theology. It is not a methods section.

Shasta Scout (February 2022) asked Bethel for medical documentation of a verified miracle. The church said retrieving before-and-after records is time-consuming and that not every person will pursue it. They could not provide documentation of a single medically verified miracle for that article. The Global Medical Research Institute had been on site since 2017 to try to verify claims to journal standards. As of that reporting, GMRI had not published a Bethel-linked medically verified miracle. They said they were investigating cases. Two GMRI case reports that had been published were not from Bethel.

Absence of a paper is not proof that nothing happened in those rooms. It is proof that Bethel has not yet cleared the bar a medical journal uses. Lourdes, for contrast, has a standing medical bureau and has certified on the order of 70 miracles across more than a century, with criteria that demand a serious documented disease and a sudden, complete recovery medicine cannot explain. That is how high the bar is when a church actually institutionalizes verification.

5. Christian researchers on mental health

This side does change when the prayer is Christian and in person, and it matches the first brief.

Boelens et al., 2009

Direct person-to-person prayer by a Christian lay minister. Six weekly hour-long sessions. Depression and anxiety improved versus controls (p less than 0.01) and held at one month. Cortisol did not move. Small, mostly women, adjunct to care.

Harold Koenig, Duke

Christian physician. Across hundreds of studies, religiosity is more often associated with less depression than more. In medically ill older patients, intrinsic religiosity predicted faster remission of depression. That is faith as a lived practice, not a remote protocol.

Craig Keener

New Testament scholar. Two-volume Miracles (2011) and later Miracles Today. Case documentation from around the world, including medical records in some instances. Not an RCT. He is explicit that most blind people prayed for are still blind. The claim is that enough well-documented cases exist to take seriously, not that healing prayer works on demand.

How to hold it

  1. Calling it Christian does not rescue the distant-prayer trials. They already were.
  2. The biblical pattern is proximal: presence, oil, hands, Jesus' name. That is a different experiment. One small field study in Clark's network measured real sensory change. It is interesting. It is not a license to skip an ENT or an ophthalmologist.
  3. Bethel and Johnson have not published the medical verification their public reputation implies. Testimonies are data of a kind. They are not the same kind as STEPP or STEP.
  4. The mental-health signal is the cleanest Christian finding, and it does not require a miracle claim. People who pray and belong do better on depression and, in the big epidemiology, live longer.
  5. A biblical view can want healing, pray for it, and still take the medicine. Paul did both.
