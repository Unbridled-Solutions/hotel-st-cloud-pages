Eagles Landing · Grand Lake

A Personal Letter

To Scott

From Stan Bullis  ·  August 27, 2026

Scott,

This closeout on Grand Lake has been a little painful for both of us. It is dragging longer than either of us intended.

I have given a lot of thought to our call yesterday. I want to make you whole. I want this to be equitable for you, for Francesca, and for me. Without you two, we would not be here. Not the land. Not the job. On balance this has been a really good partnership and a really good relationship. I wanted to share my heart a little bit.

My goal is simple. Keep our connection alive. Finish this project out strong. I know you want that too. I feel it from you.

The tension

Here is what I am struggling with, and why I keep putting deadlines on finishing. I have watched the subs slow down. Intensity dropped. Focus dropped. It started around the time Billy and Pedro went east, end of June. July and August, on balance, there has been very little work on site. We are having a hard time getting the subs back.

I hear you. You are trying to organize it when people have time, when we can work together, so we do not burn housing and extra cost. That makes sense.

On my side I see sixty days go by. I am paying to have the project sit, with not a lot of activity. Those two things are in tension. I want to save money. I also do not want to delay the project.

If I am being honest, paying $10,000 a month for GC work when we cannot get the subs back is just hard to swallow. So I am looking for a new way to close out the project effectively and quickly. I know you are doing your best, and we are doing our best. We need the guys to show up and do their best. Otherwise I do not know what I am paying for if the construction site is dark.

I do not have unlimited resources. I have enough to pay everybody out and close the job. You can see the compromises around the house. We are tightening our belts a little. I am feeling a little frustrated. I am sure you are too.

Finish strong

Among other things, the main things I am worried about are:

  • Finishing the Acacia
  • Finishing the painting and the trim, including painting and patching with the trim
  • Patching the ceiling in the basement
  • Putting all the flashing around the base of the building
  • Putting the fascia board on the dormers where the holes are

Closeout tension is real. I have done it enough times to know it is not isolated to this job. It is part of the construction process. That does not mean we leave it hanging.

How we move

If I take the $10,000 a month and divide it by 20 working days, I get $500 a day. That feels like the cleanest path forward.

That keeps us from going back and forth. It keeps things moving. It feels fair to me. I am open to discussion around it.

The same daily rate can carry forward. Closets, if we do them, are an extension, not this closeout. Snow removal. Cleaning. The miscellaneous work it takes to manage a property like that. A daily rate is the simplest path for both of us, and it would not keep feeding this frustration.

I am golfing in the tournament today. I will be up this weekend. We can talk then if you want.

Thank you.

Stan Bullis
