OpCo group playbook

How Fremont Makers and Timber run a shop channel: one company chief of staff, MC in the room for the hard calls, floor people who can ask and flag, nobody on the floor who can ship software. Companion to the BeUnbridled offer page, not a replacement.

The call Keep the dedicated company chief of staff. That is the product. Put the shop in its own Telegram group with that bot as the voice. Add MC as a second seat for software, cross-company, and “this is not ready.” Floor users talk in that group. They do not get write authority. They do not get MC in a DM. One group per company. Do not dump extra bots into Stan, MC and Lindsay.

Four seats, not a free-for-all

Name the job in the room

  • Principal. Stan, Lindsay, Justin. Can say ship it, stop it, change the tool, spend tokens, add a person.
  • Company chief of staff. Fremont bot or Timber bot. Answers shop work. Quotes, packets, cabin notes, “where is that file.” Does not deploy because a floor user sounded urgent.
  • MC. Cross-company brain and software gate. Speaks when tagged, or when the company bot would be about to break something.
  • Floor user. Chrissy and the next person like her. Uses the group as a radio. Asks, flags, pastes a photo, says the customer needs X. No credentials. No production. No “just push it.”

How to talk so two bots do not fight

Two bots in one Telegram group is a known mess (MC and Wealth Commander already taught us). It still works if you are strict about who answers.

  • Make the company bot admin in that group (delete-messages can stay off). Without admin, Telegram privacy means it only hears @mentions and replies to itself.
  • MC stays in the group with require mention. It does not narrate every quote question.
  • Both bots: exclusive bot mentions. Tagging Fremont must not also wake MC.
  • Floor default: talk in plain English. The company bot is the one that answers shop questions.
  • Software, deploy, new tool, “change the builder”: tag MC, or a principal says it. Company bot’s line is “flagged, not shipping.”
  • Bots cannot DM each other. Humans are the bridge. That is fine. The combined knowledge is in the thread, not in a bot-to-bot API.

Allow lists (this is the lock)

Floor users on the company bot only

Chrissy goes on Fremont’s numeric allow list. She does not go on default MC. If she can DM MC, she will wander into hotel labor, letters, and half the house. The group is the privilege. The DM is not.

  • Telegram needs the numeric ID. Username is not enough. Bot cannot message first. They tap the company bot once, or they just live in the group after a principal adds them.
  • Justin appends the ID to the company profile .env and restarts only that gateway. Do not bounce MC.
  • Agent cannot write .env. Do not wait on a floor user to “figure out access.”
  • If they leave the company, pull the ID the same day.

What the company CoS may do without asking

  • Answer where a quote, packet, cabin drawing, or tracker lives.
  • Fill a field the tool already has. Recalc. Show the live URL.
  • Take a photo or voice note and turn it into a written flag.
  • Tell the room what is already true in the system.
  • Say “I need Stan or Lindsay on this” when the ask is new software, a new price rule, a delete, or a credential.

What it must not do, even if the group is loud

  • Deploy, merge, rewrite the quote builder, change KV rules, rotate keys.
  • Delete jobs, wipe packets, or “clean up” production data.
  • Add a human to an allow list.
  • Speak for a different company. Fremont does not answer Timber. Timber does not answer 1887.
  • Treat urgency from the floor as authority. Urgency is information. Authority is a named principal.

How principals should use the room

  1. Let the shop talk in the shop group. Do not drag quote chatter into Stan, MC and Lindsay.
  2. If you want software, say it in one sentence. “Add overlay as a line on the quote PDF. Do not redesign the tracker.”
  3. If the floor asked for five things, pick one. The CoS should not guess priority.
  4. Approve in the group so the floor sees the decision. Then the CoS has cover.
  5. When it is done, paste the live URL in the group. That is the close. Not a long recap in three chats.

What to tell a floor user on day one

Paste this, then stop talking

This group is Fremont Makers radio. Ask anything about jobs, quotes, customers, what’s missing. Paste photos. Talk like you talk.

Fremont is the shop chief of staff. Tag it if it stays quiet. MC sits in here for software and anything that would change the tools. Do not ask MC for a cabinet quote. Do not ask Fremont to push code.

You can request a change. You cannot authorize one. If it needs building, Stan or Lindsay will say go.

Build order

  1. Fremont group first. Lindsay creates it, adds the Fremont bot, Stan, then Chrissy. Bot admin. Confirm Fremont answers a shop question.
  2. Add MC. Confirm MC stays quiet until tagged. Confirm tagging Fremont does not wake MC.
  3. Put Chrissy’s numeric ID on Fremont only. One test: she asks a real job question in the group.
  4. Timber copies the same pattern when the people are named. Do not invent a Timber floor roster.
  5. Grandpa Vaughan stays on his own box with his own short list. Do not mix that room into Fremont or Timber.

Why this is worth selling

The dedicated CoS is the asset. The group is how a company uses it without handing every employee the keys. White glove stays with the principals. Floor chatter becomes a log, not a second IT department. That split is the playbook. The offer page is the other URL.
