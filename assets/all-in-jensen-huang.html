Jensen Huang, the big points

About 47 minutes. Live audience. Chamath, Jason, Sacks.

What this hour is

Jensen’s case is not “ignore safety.” It is: safety and American leadership are a false choice, the extinction numbers are not science, and the last round of scary predictions already missed. Score them. Root-cause the real lab incidents. Keep building. Open models are how every company, not three labs, gets in the race. Superintelligence, his word, is already here in a narrow job: a car that just drives, proteins that just get screened. Not a god. A tool that beats a human at one thing.

The doomer record

  • Safety is paramount. Pause versus lead is a false choice. “Slowing down is definitely the wrong strategy.”
  • Civilizational-death percentages: “we shouldn’t, because it’s made up.” Said by researchers. Not grounded in science.
  • Score the last predictions: radiology would wipe out radiologists in five years (wrong; we need more, AI reads the scan). 90% of code AI-written in 6–12 months (wrong). 50% of entry jobs gone in 6–9 months (wrong). GPT-2 and Llama 3 too unsafe to release (wrong).
  • “We have to take account for all of the stupid predictions that were made.”
  • Whistleblowers: take them seriously. Coxin had courage. The scientific prophecy in the same post is a different thing.
  • Labs are going from research to engineering, company, culture, and product at once. Hair on fire. That is not the same as “the model is out of control.”
  • Nvidia culture he named: no political discourse at work. Take it home. Company is bipartisan. America succeeds.

Regulation, RSI, where danger lives

  • Regulation should solve actual problems. So far the actual incidents came from frontier labs, because they have the compute. A high schooler or a startup does not.
  • First move: root-cause like an engineer. What happened. What we change. Lock it in (sandbox, runtime, monitors). He would bet the labs can prevent a repeat.
  • The other story (“we analyzed it and we still don’t know, please help”) he finds unlikely. If it were true, send engineers in.
  • Third-party evals, like financial auditors. More than one, so one shop does not get captured.
  • Pacing and pausing: voluntary, and only if a lab believes it is out of control.
  • RSI is not a demon. It is in-context learning, skills, reflection, reinforcement learning, synthetic data, LoRA. You can RSI all day inside the company. You still eval the product before it ships. No regression.
  • Chinese lab (Zhipu / GLM) raising and naming RSI as a priority: he treats that as using AI to build AI, which everyone is already doing in some degree. The phrase is being used to weaponize the work.

Open models / Hugging Face / China

  • The world needs closed and open. Closed models are bottled water. Tap water is free. Use the right water in the right place.
  • Jensen’s number, not ours: last six months, $400 billion of venture into AI-native companies, 80% on open models. Without open weights those founders cannot build a dream that is different from the labs’.
  • Winning the race is not three tech companies winning. It is every company, industry, researcher, teacher, student, startup.
  • Jason framed Hugging Face as more than a transaction. Jensen did not walk it back. Open stack also for self-driving and biology, built because customers needed it.
  • Does it matter if the open model came from China? Once you download it, it is yours. Fork it. Improve it. Same as Linux and Kubernetes.
  • The last industrial revolution’s inventors (Maxwell, Volta, Ampère) were not American. America exploited the technology socially better than anyone. That is the race he wants again: who uses it best.
  • China’s public story is practical. Economy and society. Not end-of-civilization groups. “If it was true, we ought to spend more time doing something about it than worrying a bunch of people who can’t do anything about it.”

Trump on speaker

  • Not planned. Jensen’s phone. Jason puts him on the mic.
  • Calls the doom story a hoax. “The robots are not going to be taking over the world.” Still: be careful, do not stop the industry while you study how to destroy it.
  • Data centers: “the oil of the next 20, 25 years.” Bigger than the internet. Communities that were dying now have them and are wealthy. Google wanting Finland because it could not get permitting here: he is not happy about that.
  • “Whoever wins AI wins.”
  • His claim on the call: $20 trillion of investment into the country in one year versus under $1 trillion over four years under Biden. That is his number. We did not check it here.
  • Jensen after the call, what he wanted to say on the line: AI is creating an enormous number of jobs. Re-industrialize. Energy first. Without energy there is no industrial growth. Be better listeners in small towns as the data centers go in (he named Abbott in Texas on that).

Nvidia’s posture

  • Look across the whole stack for bottlenecks. Upstream they did that years early (Corning, Lumentum, TSMC, memory). Now downstream: land, power, shell (Cloverleaf), financing with BlackRock and Goldman. Chamath called it the bank of AI.
  • A year and a half ago the only model they ran was OpenAI. Now Meta, Grok, Gemini, Anthropic scaling, plus newer labs. He’d rather help everybody succeed than take a slice.
  • Strategy in his words: go up as far as we need to, and as low as possible. Invent what the ecosystem cannot invent (cuDNN, Megatron Core), then let a thousand flowers bloom.
  • “Surprisingly uncompetitive.” Happy with five hyperscalers. NeoClouds matter because hyperscalers plan once a year and the market is too volatile; regional clouds can see land and power that Seattle and Palo Alto cannot.
  • They build the stack when customers need it and cannot: reasoning self-driving (Alpamayo) so every car, truck, van does not have to invent autonomy; protein models (ESM2, ESMFold, OpenFold, AlphaFold 2, Proteina) because Lilly and Merck need them.
  • Elon’s Terafab: “If anybody could do it, he can.” You cannot discourage Elon once he decides. China advanced lithography: Jensen said 2030, and for him two or three years is a click.

Superintelligence, his definition

  • AGI: “I think we’re already there.”
  • Superintelligence too, if you take a narrow segment. “My self-driving car, I don’t want you to make me an omelet. I just want you to drive the car.” Better than a human. About 1/10 the accident rate, as they said it.
  • Same for synthesizing and screening proteins.
  • Close: the future is great, tone down the drama, bring all of America. “I like it.”

For Unbridled

Clarity, not a pause memo

  • Fear drops when you score the last prediction. Same as our room: the idea stays, risk gets a number, two or three moves drop the number.
  • Narrow superintelligence is the useful frame. Not “AI will run Unbridled.” One job, done better than a person, with a human still on the guest, the quote, or the payroll.
  • Open versus closed: use the right water. Guest files, payroll, Cloudbeds: closed, scoped, human look. CAD, briefs, labor drafts, shop sketches: open is fine if the human still ships it.
  • RSI in our language is skills, memory, overnight runs. Still eval before it leaves the shop.
