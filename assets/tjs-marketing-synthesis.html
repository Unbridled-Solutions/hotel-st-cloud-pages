TJ's Log & Timber — Digital Marketing Synthesis

Everything from the Timber group thread (Sept 17 – Oct 7), Jim's plan, Stan's positioning calls, Search Console, and a direct pull of the live Google Ads account (172-455-1760). What's working, what's broken, what we don't know, who the customer actually is — and the agent team to run it. Built as the foundation for the new digital marketing strategy.

The call Google Ads is working as a brand amplifier and failing as a demand engine. The statewide campaign earns an 18% CTR but most conversions are people who already knew the name. The 15 micro-geo campaigns are structurally starved — eleven of them have spent under $12 each in 30 days, and the flagship corridors (Bailey, Hwy 34) got 101 impressions combined. Meanwhile the biggest lead source in the whole business — 5–10 walk-ins a day at the mill — has no capture process, and the landers converted ~590 paid clicks into roughly 2 tracked email clicks. The fix is not more campaigns. It's consolidation, conversion plumbing, and a team of agents running a weekly loop instead of ad-hoc tinkering.

2 · What's Working

The brand has real local gravity

FactSearch Console: "tjs log and timber" and variants = 600+ organic clicks at 28–55% CTR, position ~1. People hear about TJ's on 285, from neighbors, from the yard — then Google the name. The mill, the drive-by, the reputation is the top of funnel. Jim was right: "the property should be one of our best advertisements."

Ad relevance is genuinely strong

Fact18% CTR statewide and 14% on Bailey are exceptional numbers (non-brand search ads typically run 2–5%). The copy Stan numbered — "Log homes | Hand selected Colorado logs | Milled in Bailey" — resonates. When the ad shows, people click. The problem has never been the ads. It's what happens before (auction volume) and after (the lander).

The plumbing got built fast

FactIn three weeks: 6+ landers on cabins.tjslogandtimber.com with UTM tagging and an A/B/C lander split, GA4 + Google Ads conversion tags, phone/email click events, a HubSpot form event, a daily 6am ads cron, a cloud dashboard, and negatives shipped across all 15 geos on Oct 7. Most companies this size never get this far. The rails exist — they're just carrying almost no freight yet.

The team knows what it doesn't know

FactJonathan, Oct 3: "I'd pay $10–20 per click if it's someone clearly wanting to build a cabin. I just don't want to pay $3 for searches that are nothing to do with it." That is the correct economics — on a $500k+ cabin sale, CAC can be thousands. The instinct is right; the account structure fights it.

3 · What's Broken

Fifteen micro-geo campaigns can't win auctions

FactEleven geos spent <$12 each in 30 days. Parker, Lone Tree, Greenwood Village: zero clicks. Each campaign has its own budget, its own learning, and a pond of a few thousand households searching for log cabins a handful of times a month. Google's auction system needs volume to learn; fifteen $15/day silos never get it. Bailey's CPC hit $13–20 because a starving campaign bids desperate. Jonathan spotted it himself on Sept 30: "we've only spent $60 in 2 weeks." The structure is the bug.

590 clicks → ~2 tracked conversions. The funnel leaks at the lander.

Fact$2,172 bought 590 clicks and the conversion actions show 2 email clicks and 0 form fills. Either the landers don't convert, or conversions happen untracked (people just call the number from the website later), or both. Jonathan Sept 24: "we've had click throughs but no conversions... should we edit the landers?" — correct question, never systematically answered. The Oct 6 catalogue-download idea is the right fix and is half-built.

The statewide campaign buys the wrong "kit" buyer

Fact"log home kit" keyword: $418, 117 clicks, zero conversions. Search terms include "small cabin kits for under $25,000", "cabins for sale under $200,000", "diy cabin kits", "cabin in a box." TJ's sells kit + mill-to-square + stack at ~$400/SF build economics — not a $25k mail-order shed. Roughly $500–600 of the month's spend went to price-mismatched or wrong-product searches (modular, amish, prefab, for-sale listings). The Oct 7 negatives helped; the keyword set still skews bargain.

No lead definition, no CRM loop, no revenue attribution

Consensus, unresolvedJim's plan (Sept 17/21): lead source → qualified → proposal → deposit → closed → revenue. "Ultimately we need to know which channels actually generate sales, not just clicks." That pipeline still doesn't exist. HubSpot is mentioned ten times in the thread and wired to nothing. The 5–10 daily walk-ins — potentially 150–300 leads/month, dwarfing anything Google produces — have no capture process. Katie's institutional knowledge lives only in Katie's head.

Quality control burned trust

FactOct 4: a live lander hero image shipped with internal label "Castle Pines Cherry Hills Village Version C" visible to prospects; unapproved copy ("Leave Friday. Be in the logs.") appeared on South Denver; the catalogue draft leaked internal notes and single-word sentences. Stan: "now I feel like I need to go through each one page by page." The housing-certification disapproval (age-exclusion triggered RESTRICTED_HOUSING, Sept 19) cost two days. Every asset needs a brand gate before it goes live — this is exactly what the reviewer agent is for.

Organic non-brand is invisible, Facebook never launched

FactGSC: "log home builders colorado" position 80, "colorado log home builders" position 72, "log cabin kits colorado" position 75. TJ's does not exist organically for any non-brand term — every non-brand visitor must be bought. The Sept 18 blog/SEO initiative stalled. Meta ads were requested twice, blocked on token/Page access, and dropped. Two whole channels sit at zero.

4 · What We Don't Know

  • What a lead is worth. No tracked path from click → inquiry → proposal → deposit. Until one closed sale is attributed, every budget debate is theology.
  • Whether the wealth-zip thesis is true. Opinion"Rich people in Cherry Hills build mountain second homes" is plausible and untested — 2 conversions from those geos were both brand searches. The counter-thesis: cabin intent lives in search terms, not neighborhoods, and statewide + smart keywords already catches Cherry Hills residents.
  • Whether Dallas works. 92 impressions is noise. Needs 90 days and a real conversion goal, or kill it.
  • What walk-ins convert at. If 5–10/day is real and even 2% buy, walk-ins out-produce every digital channel combined. Nobody has counted.
  • What the former owner knew. Jim: decades of profitable sales via reputation and relationships. Where did customers actually come from? Nobody has asked him yet.
  • Phone call volume and quality. 9 ad calls all-time; no recording, no duration data, no notes on whether any became opportunities.

6 · Recommendations

HighR1 — Consolidate 13 Denver-metro geos into ONE "Front Range Wealth" campaign.
One campaign, all the affluent zips as location targets, one shared $40–50/day budget, the proven keyword set, lander A/B/C rotation preserved via ad groups. Keep Bailey/285 separate (it's a walk-in radius play, different intent). Park Hwy 34 (10 impressions = no fish in that pond; the Grand County buyer gets caught by statewide). Keep Dallas as the lone out-of-state test with a 90-day verdict date. Result: 17 campaigns → 4. Google finally gets enough signal to optimize, CPCs normalize, and reporting becomes readable.
HighR2 — Make the catalogue download the primary conversion, and wire it end-to-end.
Finish the PDF catalogue (Jonathan's Oct 6 ask, needs the full remake he described). Gate it behind name + email + phone + "own land?" on every lander. Fire a Google Ads conversion on submit, push the contact into HubSpot with UTM source, and trigger a same-day follow-up. This single fix turns 590 anonymous clicks/month into a lead list and finally gives Google a conversion signal to bid toward.
HighR3 — Split brand from non-brand in the statewide campaign.
Brand terms ("tjs log and timber" etc.) into a $5/day exact-match campaign — they convert at pennies and currently pollute the stats. Rebuild non-brand around builder intent ("log home builders colorado", "custom log homes") and away from bargain-kit terms; add price-tier negatives (under $25000, cheap, diy, tiny home, modular, prefab — partially done Oct 7). Then the "is statewide actually working?" question answers itself.
HighR4 — Build the lead ledger before spending another dollar on expansion.
One HubSpot pipeline: source → qualified → toured Bailey → proposal → deposit → closed, with revenue. Every walk-in, call, form, and catalogue download goes in. This is Jim's plan, it was right on Sept 17, and it's the yardstick every agent below reports against. Pair it with a one-page walk-in capture script at the mill (Katie/front desk, tablet or paper, 60 seconds).
MedR5 — Launch the GC / realtor / architect program as email + packet, not ads.
Stan's force-rank #2 and #3 and Jim's referral-network plan both point here, and nothing shipped. Named list of 2–3 custom builders per mountain county, the trade packet, a Bailey mill tour invitation. The HNW buyer is reached through these people, not through Bow Mar radius ads.
MedR6 — Start the SEO/content engine against the GSC gap.
Position 70–90 on every non-brand Colorado term is free demand being handed to competitors. One quality page per cluster ("log home builders colorado", "log cabin kits colorado", "colorado mountain cabin cost"), real photos, the catalogue CTA. Revive the Sept 18 blog initiative with a brand gate. 6–12 month payoff, costs almost nothing but agent time.
MedR7 — Set the monthly number and a kill rule.
Recommend $2,500/mo all-in for 90 days: ~$1,100 statewide non-brand, ~$150 brand, ~$900 Front Range Wealth, ~$350 Bailey + Dallas. Kill rule: any campaign with 90 days, $300+ spend, and zero qualified leads gets parked — automatically reported by the analyst, decided by Jonathan.
LowR8 — Unblock Meta, but only after R2.
Facebook/Instagram is the right discovery channel for cabin dreaming (visual, second-home fantasy). It was blocked on token/Page access in September. Don't relitigate it until the catalogue funnel exists — discovery traffic without a lead magnet is the same leak at a higher volume.
LowR9 — Interview the former owner. Count the walk-ins for 30 days.
Two cheap research moves that could reorder every priority above. If walk-ins are real at 5–10/day, the 285 corridor physical program (signage, mobile cabin, the premises themselves) outranks all digital spend.

7 · The Agent Team

Five agents, three humans, one weekly loop. All agents run on the Timber profile (the account, credentials, and daily cron already live there), with MC as cross-company orchestrator and escalation path. Cheap models for the daily grind, frontier models for judgment.

1. Ads Analyst

Daily · 6am MT cron (exists — gets upgraded) · read-only · cheap model

Pulls the account every morning: spend, clicks, search terms, waste, auction share, conversion events. Flags anomalies (like Bailey's $20 CPC cap, which sat unnoticed) and proposes negatives. Hotel St. Cloud dashboard format, TJ's branding — exactly what Stan asked for on Sept 18 and 20. Never mutates the account.

2. Ads Strategist

Weekly · frontier model · executes only what Jonathan approves

Owns structure: consolidation (R1), budgets, bids, keyword changes, the 90-day kill rule. Produces one weekly brief — "here are the 3 changes I recommend and why" — Jonathan replies "do 1 and 3" and it executes. Ends the ad-hoc tinkering loop that created 17 campaigns.

3. Copywriter / Lander Builder

On demand · frontier model · everything passes the brand gate

RSAs, landers, the catalogue, blog posts. Carries the accumulated voice rules from the thread: no round logs, no "milled to square," no single-word sentences, no internal notes in client-facing work, Stan numbers headlines, "it feels AI" means rewrite. Nothing ships without the reviewer pass — the Version-C leak never happens again.

4. Brand Reviewer

Gate on every asset · the existing unbridled-brand-reviewer, loaded with TJ's rules

Checks every lander, ad, and PDF against the locked positioning (kit + mill-to-square + stack publicly, no turnkey, no mill dollars, no "best") and against Google housing-policy pitfalls (no age exclusions — that's what killed the ads on Sept 19). Also checks for leaked internal labels. Cheap insurance against the two incidents that burned the most trust.

5. Lead Desk

Daily · watches HubSpot + conversion events + call log

The missing piece. Every form fill, catalogue download, and ad call becomes a HubSpot contact with source attribution. Daily digest to the group: "2 new leads yesterday — catalogue download from Castle Rock (owns land, Grand County), ad call 3:40 from Bailey." Monthly: the funnel report Jim specified — leads → qualified → proposal → deposit → revenue by channel. This is the agent that eventually answers "who actually buys."

The humans

  • Jonathan — ads owner. Approves the strategist's weekly brief. Sets budget. (Matches his "revise marketing plans — JW — in progress" assignment from the Sept 23 turnaround list.)
  • Stan — brand and positioning. Numbers headlines, approves the catalogue, decides Dallas/Hwy 34 kills.
  • Justin — infrastructure: Meta token when the time comes, HubSpot API access for the Lead Desk, any new credentials.
  • Jim — the offline half: GC/realtor list, walk-in process with Katie, former-owner interview. His Sept 21 note already volunteered for exactly this: "I will test it, catch walk-ins, name ten people in Park and Grand... Ads stay yours."

The cadence

  • Daily 6am — Analyst scorecard + Lead Desk digest (one combined message, not two).
  • Weekly Monday — Strategist brief: 3 recommendations max, each one approvable with a single word.
  • Monthly — Funnel report against the lead ledger. Budget decision. Kill-rule verdicts.
